The Prestige Plan

Replacing the flat multiplier with a points economy,
a cost-reduction store, milestones & cards.

The Shift

Today, prestige does exactly one thing: a permanent payout multiplier. The plan turns it into a multi-track permanent-progression layer — the real heart of the idle loop.

Today

Cash out → earn High-Roller Points = floor(√(lifetime / 10000)). Points feed a single flat boost: ×(1 + 0.25·HRP). That's the whole system. No decisions inside it.

The plan

Points feed two competing sinks — a cost-reduction store and card packs — plus count-gated milestones. "What am I doing this prestige?" becomes a real choice.

Flagged for removal alongside this: the totalEverWon atom, which only existed to feed the old multiplier.

1 Points currency Pinned

The formula is generalized from today's square root into a tunable power curve.

points = floor((lifetimeChips / K) ^ exp)

exp starts at 1.0 (linear). Push it to 1.05–1.1 later to reward plateau-pushing. Deliberately ship it undertuned — buffing is easier than nerfing.

2 Shared-spend tension Pinned

Points are spent on a store AND on card packs from one pool. They compete. That competition is the main strategic decision the whole layer creates — it's the point, not a side effect.

3 The store: cost-reduction tracks Pinned

One track per upgrade. Each level cuts that upgrade's chip cost by a slice.

effectiveCost = baseCost / (1 + r · k)

r = track level · k = per-upgrade scalar. Divisor math is deliberate: it never hits zero, so everything stays buyable. UI shows it as a percent ("−45% bet cost").

Double duty: (a) accelerate the next run back to where you were, and (b) let you push deeper into the exponential cost curve than an un-prestiged player ever could.

4 Milestones Pinned

Unlocks gated on prestige count, not on spend. Permanent versions of things already in the base game.

  • Permanent autospin (some tier)
  • First extraReel / extraRow
  • Possibly: a new symbol, a new payline tier

5 Card packs Pinned

The deepest permanent layer. Bought with points; competes with the store.

Paylines-as-cards is the anchoring idea. Cards may also govern symbol payouts, wild probability, and more.

Cards survive even a future meta-prestige ("reset everything but your cards"). They're the bottom of the stack.

The framing that matters

Prestige is explicitly one stepping stone, not the top of the tree. The game expects more reset layers above it — each layer's currency is "the points of the previous layer's runs." Build prestige so it can later become a mid-game layer. Don't architect it as terminal.

Still open Decide

① Caps philosophy

Stay capped, or go uncapped + rely on the cost curve? Tentative split: numerical upgrades (bet, multi, passive) uncapped & reductions extend them; physical-cap upgrades (autospin delay, tick rate, reel/row) stay capped & prestige raises the ceiling. Might feel muddier — pending playtest.

② Point-to-upgrade ratio

At ~1,000 pts/prestige, does one reduction cost ~50 (many buys, commit to a track) or ~200 (few buys, spread thin)?

③ Card depth & rate

Acquisition rate and mechanics beyond paylines — both unresolved.

Suggested phasing

Each phase is shippable on its own.

Phase 1 — Points + store

Generalize the points formula; build the cost-reduction store and fold r·k into cost math. Replaces the flat multiplier entirely.
